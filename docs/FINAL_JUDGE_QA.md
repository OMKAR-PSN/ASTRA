# ASTRA CARE — Judge questions and defensible answers

Audit date: 17 November 2026. Educational prototype; not a flight-ready medical system.

1. **Why is it necessary?** It explores a crew-friendly way to record observations, understand deterministic comparisons, and keep records available when communications are unavailable. It does not establish that existing mission systems lack these capabilities.
2. **How is it different from existing astronaut monitoring?** Our contribution is the integrated interaction prototype: guided manual entry, explainable comparisons, historical replay, bundled learning, and an explicit local report queue. We have not conducted a competitive or operational validation study.
3. **Are the measurements real?** No. The built-in crew and histories are fictional deterministic fixtures. Demonstrations must use synthetic manual inputs and be labeled as such.
4. **Does it connect to a smartwatch or NFC device?** No. QR identification selects a fictional fixture; contactless-card identification is simulated. No wearable data ingestion or real NFC read is implemented.
5. **Is the assessment clinical AI?** No. It uses deterministic comparisons and rule-based explanations. Adaptive questions are conditional prompts, not a machine-learning diagnosis.
6. **Why 10%?** It is an existing synthetic demonstration threshold, not a clinically validated cutoff. Percentage changes across different quantities, especially Celsius temperature, are not a clinical risk model. We preserve the rule for reproducibility, not clinical endorsement.
7. **Can it work without Earth communication?** Already-loaded web flows use local data. The production web service worker can support cached offline launches after installation completes. A first visit on a never-cached device still needs the app files. Native bundles compile, but this audit has no phone execution evidence.
8. **How do reports reach ground?** Explicit confirmation stores an immutable snapshot in the local outbox. A separate send/retry action posts it to the configured simulated HTTP receiver. This is not a space communication link.
9. **How do you prevent false acknowledgements?** The client validates response status, report digest, payload hash, reference, and timestamp before storing an acknowledgement. The receiver persists receipts and supports idempotent requests. These checks do not authenticate a malicious receiver; there is no cryptographic signature or production identity system.
10. **Can it diagnose a condition?** No. It displays observations, calculations, limitations, and non-diagnostic educational context. It does not determine urgency or prescribe treatment.
11. **How is private data protected?** A restricted summary omits health details; an opaque visual shield conceals the app and stops guided speech. Records are plain local JSON in IndexedDB or SQLite. This is not encryption, authorization, or protection against device/storage access.
12. **Is QR identification secure authentication?** No. It selects one fictional identity for a session-only demonstration. It does not prove who is holding the device.
13. **What happens when values are missing?** Missing values remain missing. Available readings can be saved, unavailable comparisons are identified, and unanswered symptoms differ from an explicit no-symptoms response. The app must not invent readings.
14. **What is implemented versus simulated?** Implemented: local storage adapters, forms, validation, rule-based calculations, replay, knowledge, speech controls, privacy UI, queue, and receiver protocol. Simulated: crew identity, mission context, historical fixtures, link indicator, and ground endpoint. Hardware sensing, clinical validation, real mission communications, authentication, and operational authorization are absent.
15. **What would real deployment require?** Crew and clinician validation, appropriate measurement/decision rules, hazard analysis, verified hardware interfaces, authenticated encrypted communications, ownership and access controls, retention/recovery procedures, rigorous native/accessibility testing, reliability qualification, and the applicable mission approval process. Passing prototype tests is not flight certification.

NASA's [five hazards overview](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazards/) supports the spaceflight context; its [cardiovascular research overview](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/cardiovascular-health-in-microgravity/) supports general adaptation context. Neither validates this application's threshold or certifies this prototype.
